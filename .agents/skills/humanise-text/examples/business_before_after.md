## AFTER (human-rewritten)

### We built TaskFlow AI because spreadsheet-based project management almost killed our last product launch

Two years ago, our 14-person team missed a client deadline by three weeks. Not because anyone was lazy. Because our project tracker -- a colour-coded Google Sheet with 47 tabs -- couldn't tell us that two engineers were assigned to the same blocked task while a designer sat idle. We built TaskFlow AI to fix that specific problem.

The core feature is automatic task routing. The system looks at three things: what skills a task requires, who on your team has those skills, and who actually has bandwidth right now. It pulls availability from your calendar, current task load from the board, and skill tags from each person's profile. Then it suggests assignments. You can override every one. Most users override about 15% of suggestions in the first month, dropping to 4% by month three.

It connects to 200+ tools because we had to. Nobody wanted another standalone app. If your team lives in Slack, tasks show up in Slack. If your PMs live in Jira, TaskFlow syncs bidirectionally with Jira. We built the Salesforce connector because three early customers asked for it in the same week.

The part I'm most proud of is the deadline forecasting. TaskFlow tracks how long similar tasks actually took in the past -- not how long someone estimated they'd take -- and flags projects that are likely to slip. It caught a two-week delay on a client project for us last quarter, early enough that we reassigned resources and delivered on time. It's wrong sometimes. Roughly 80% accuracy on delay predictions, which is better than gut instinct but not magic.

After 18 months and about 340 teams using the platform, here's what the data shows: median time-to-completion dropped 22%. Missed deadlines fell by a third. One number I didn't expect -- support tickets about "who's doing what" dropped 60%, which tells me the real problem was always visibility, not effort.

We charge $12/user/month. Free 14-day trial, no credit card required. If your team is under 5 people and your projects aren't complex, you probably don't need this. A shared Trello board will do fine.
