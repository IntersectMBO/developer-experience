## Purpose

This guide helps newcomers find a practical route into Cardano development and open-source contribution. It is written with particular attention to African developers with limited access to established developer networks, women entering blockchain, people learning in a second language, people with disabilities, and contributors working with limited equipment, connectivity, or time.

These groups have different needs. Choose the steps and participation options that fit your circumstances. You do not need to share personal information about your background to use this guide or ask a technical question.

The starting goal is simple: understand where you want to contribute, complete one manageable task, and know where to ask for help.

---

## 1. Understand Where You Are Starting

Cardano is a blockchain platform on which developers can build applications. Its ecosystem also includes people maintaining software, writing documentation, testing tools, translating resources, and supporting communities.

Intersect is a member-based organisation within that ecosystem. The Developer Experience Working Group provides a place to learn about development tools, discuss difficulties, and explore contribution opportunities.

Start with the [Cardano Developer Portal](https://developers.cardano.org/) for development resources and the [DevEx Get Started page](https://devex.intersectmbo.org/docs/getting-started) for orientation and learning paths.

Some terms you will encounter:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Repository | A project's files and change history, often hosted on GitHub. |
| Issue | A place to report a problem, suggest an improvement, or discuss a task. |
| Pull request (PR) | A proposed change submitted for a project's maintainers to review. |
| Maintainer | Someone responsible for reviewing contributions and looking after a project. |
| Wallet | Software used to manage blockchain accounts and authorise transactions. |
| Smart contract | Code that checks whether a blockchain action follows defined rules. |
| Testnet | A separate blockchain network used to practise and test applications. |

## 2. Choose One Starting Path

Your first contribution can build on skills you already have. A formal qualification or previous blockchain project is not needed to begin reading public resources or identifying improvements.

| Your starting point | Suggested first task | A useful first result |
|---|---|---|
| New to programming or blockchain | Read one introductory page and record a confusing term or missing explanation. | A specific documentation question or suggested clarification. |
| Comfortable with writing or translation | Improve a short explanation, or ask maintainers whether a translation is wanted. | A small documentation change or agreed translation task. |
| Web or application developer | Choose a beginner example from the Developer Portal and follow its prerequisites. | A running example, or a clear report of the step that failed. |
| Experienced with testing, systems, or infrastructure | Read a relevant repository's README and contribution guide. | A reproduced issue, test improvement, or agreed starter task. |

Begin with one resource and one task. You can move between paths as your interests develop.

## 3. Adapt the Learning Process to Your Circumstances

### Limited connectivity, equipment, or budget

Start with public written documentation. Save a page for offline reading when you have connectivity, and keep questions in a local note. Session notes can be easier to access than a full recording.

If you are using a phone or shared computer, begin with reading, reviewing explanations, or preparing an issue. Check the hardware and download requirements before choosing a coding exercise. Running a full Cardano node is not a prerequisite for documentation work.

The public resources linked here can be read without purchasing Intersect membership. Check access requirements separately for member services or community channels. Before using a hosted development tool, check its costs and limits.

### Learning in a second language

Keep a short glossary with the original technical term and an explanation in your preferred language. When asking a question, include the original command or error message so others can identify the problem.

If you use machine translation, check commands, code, and technical terms against the original. Before contributing a translation, agree its scope with maintainers and request review from someone comfortable with both the language and the subject.

### Accessibility needs

Choose written instructions when audio or video is difficult to use. If a resource depends on an image, request a text explanation of the relevant steps. Ask the session organiser about captions, accessible materials, or another way to submit questions before attending; availability may vary.

You can describe the format you need without sharing a diagnosis. For example: “Could you provide these instructions as text so I can follow them with my screen reader?”

### Limited time or access to developer networks

Use short sessions with a single objective, such as reading a page or preparing a question. Written issues and discussions let you participate across time zones and around work, study, or caring responsibilities.

If you are entering a community where few participants share your background, you can begin with a written question or a small contribution. Ask a facilitator whether a newcomer introduction or peer connection is available. You do not have to attend an in-person event to start learning.

## 4. Complete Your First Contribution

A small documentation improvement is a practical starting exercise. It lets you learn the contribution process without installing a blockchain development environment.

1. Open the [developer-experience repository](https://github.com/IntersectMBO/developer-experience) and read its [contribution guidelines](https://github.com/IntersectMBO/developer-experience/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md).
2. Choose a page you have used. Identify one concrete improvement: an unexplained term, a broken link, or a missing instruction.
3. Check existing issues to see whether the problem is already being discussed. For a larger change or translation, ask maintainers about the scope before starting.
4. Sign in to GitHub if you want to submit an issue or change. Describe the page, the difficulty, and what would make it clearer.
5. If you are ready to edit, follow the repository's contribution workflow and submit a small pull request. Explain what changed and how you checked it.
6. Read reviewer feedback and respond when you can. Ask for clarification if a requested change is unclear.

For example, replace “the instructions are confusing” with: “The setup page uses the term SDK before explaining it. Could we define it as a software development kit and explain which one this example uses?”

Your first result may be a useful issue or a submitted change. Acceptance depends on maintainer review; a pending review does not prevent you from continuing to learn.

If you choose a coding exercise instead, follow its specified tool versions and network instructions. Use testnet funds for testnet exercises and keep wallet recovery phrases, private keys, and API credentials out of screenshots and public questions.

## 5. Ask for Help and Stay Connected

Choose a channel that matches your question:

- **Project-specific problem:** use the repository's issue template or the support route listed in its README.
- **Focused technical question:** use [Cardano Stack Exchange](https://cardano.stackexchange.com), including enough detail for someone to reproduce the problem.
- **Broader ecosystem discussion:** explore the [Cardano Forum](https://forum.cardano.org).
- **Learning sessions and contribution guidance:** check the [DevEx Working Group page](https://devex.intersectmbo.org/docs/working-group) for session information and materials.
- **Mentorship and informal guidance:** introduce yourself in the **Developer Experience Watercooler** on Intersect Discord. Share what you want to learn or build, and ask whether a developer advocate or community member can help you choose a first task.

The [DevEx Get Started page](https://devex.intersectmbo.org/docs/getting-started) lists these community resources and explains access to Intersect's Discord. Check current session details before joining.

You can use this short question format:

> I am trying to: [your goal].
>
> I followed: [link to the guide and the step].
>
> I expected: [the result].
>
> What happened: [error message or difficulty, with sensitive information removed].
>
> My setup is: [relevant tool versions and operating system].
>
> I would find this helpful: [a written explanation, a smaller example, or clarification of a term].

For mentorship, start with a specific request in the Watercooler, such as help choosing a learning path or reviewing a small contribution:

> Hello, I am new to Cardano and have some experience with [your skills or interests]. I would like to [your goal]. Could someone help me choose a first task or point me towards a suitable learning resource? I prefer [written replies or another suitable format].

Support depends on community members' availability. Agree on the communication channel and a manageable next step with anyone offering help. If you cannot access Discord, use the public question channels above or check the DevEx website for session participation details.

## 6. Participate Respectfully

The DevEx repository's [Code of Conduct](https://github.com/IntersectMBO/developer-experience/blob/main/CODE-OF-CONDUCT.md) sets expectations for respectful participation and covers harassment and discrimination. It identifies [oso@intersectmbo.org](mailto:oso@intersectmbo.org) as the reporting contact.

Use that private route for conduct concerns involving this community. Other projects may have their own reporting contacts. Avoid posting another person's private information in public issues or discussions.

When helping another newcomer, explain unfamiliar terms, focus feedback on the work, and ask before sharing their name, image, or personal story. Let people choose how much of their background they disclose.

## 7. Your First-Week Checklist

Use this at your own pace; the week is a suggested starting period.

- [ ] Read one introductory resource and note unfamiliar terms.
- [ ] Choose a learning path and a task that fits your time and equipment.
- [ ] Identify a suitable place to ask questions.
- [ ] Read the project's contribution guidelines and Code of Conduct.
- [ ] Complete one small action: report a documentation gap, propose an edit, or try a beginner example.
- [ ] Record what worked, what blocked you, and your next step.
